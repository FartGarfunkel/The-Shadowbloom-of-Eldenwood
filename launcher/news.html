Alpha 0.1.0.3 — Roads, Gates and the Wider World October 2026

The biggest update yet: the whole tabletop rulebook, the lands beyond the first campaign, towns that live and breathe, and a story that doesn't stop where it used to.

  • The full rulebook: every SRD 5.2 spell (339 of them), weapon, armour and magic item, with rarity and attunement; feats, epic boons and multiclassing; spells you choose and prepare yourself; conditions on the HUD with their timers.
  • Subclasses: at 3rd level, choose one of three for your class: the SRD's own, and two of Eldenwood's (the Path of the Rootbound, the College of the Rootsong, the Lantern Domain, the Warden of the Watch, the Way of the Still Water, the Oath of the Veil, the Smuggler of Saltwind, Crystal Sorcery, the School of Resonance and more). Heroes past 3rd level are asked to choose.
  • Six new species from the campaign's lands: the feline Sunmane, the bird-folk Kestrin, the treefolk Mossborn, the crystal Lumini, the drowned Tidecursed and the shadow-touched Veilborn, each with their own looks and a feature of their own. And four backgrounds from the towns.
  • Monsters of the SRD, where they belong: goblins and owlbears in the woods, the dead in the dark, scorpions on the Salt Road, sahuagin on the coast, and many more, each with a body and a signature move.
  • The wider world: Saltwind Harbor on the western coast and what's beneath its waters, the Plains of Silent Stones, the Crystal Caverns under Mount Cinder, Redrock Haven on the Salt Road, Duskwood, and more of Stonebridge and Oakhaven, with their people and their quests. And the story goes further than it did.
  • Roads: every town and every place of the story is joined by roads and trails, paved near the towns, with bridges over the Blackroot River and signposts at the junctions.
  • Gates: walled towns have real gates now, great timber doors or an iron portcullis that you watch swing and rattle. They shut at dusk with the evening bell and open at dawn; the watch lets you in at night. Every gate is named for its direction, with signs saying where the road goes. Maps have a compass rose, and Eldon's map has a map in it.
  • Towns that live: flags, banners, bunting and washing that move in the wind, shop signs swinging over the doors; townsfolk with a day of work, an evening at the tavern or on the square's benches, and a day off; market days and chapel days. Townspeople in the big towns no longer stand frozen in place.
  • On the road: things happen between the towns, and you see them coming: tracks, smoke, an overturned cart, a frightened traveller. Spot the signs and you catch them at their camp; miss them and it's an ambush.
  • Dungeons: traps to spot, step round or disarm, and locked chests to pick or force, with the rolls shown.
  • Sellswords and houses: hire a shield-bearer, bowman or healer at the inn; buy a house in Stonebridge, Oakhaven or Saltwind Harbor.
  • The day ends when you rest: in a campaign world the evening holds until the party sleeps, like the end of a session.
  • Everything in 3D: weapons, armour, gear and magic items are shaped models in your hand, on the ground, on racks, and as their pictures in your bag. Shields are held like shields, and you block with them (hold Use).
  • Distant terrain shows the roads, the town walls and the true colour of the land. Clocks run clockwise. A controller looks around more smoothly, and shop lists scroll. The tabletop's packs open and can be worn. Fate finishes what she's saying before she goes.
  • New worlds get the roads, gates and towns' decorations. For a world from an older version, "Upgrade terrain" in its settings brings them to the land you haven't explored yet.

Alpha 0.1.0.2 — Into the Eldenwood October 2026

The whole campaign is here, from the first letter to the last page, along with real RPG towns, live-action fights, a new main menu and heroes (and townsfolk) in 3D.

  • The full story: three acts of the Shadowbloom of Eldenwood, with the people, towns, letters, dreams, side quests and places of the campaign, and more than one way for it to end. The test chapter is gone.
  • Your hero first: the main menu is a stage, with your hero standing on it. You make your hero before you make a world, and every new world starts with them: no character sheet in the way. The hero maker is a stage too, the figure big in the middle, and you can turn it round.
  • Heroes in 3D: real hair (long, ponytails, braids, crests, curls, topknots), beards (down to a proper long wizard's beard; wizards start with one), pointed ears, horns, tusks and tails, hoods and hats (the wizard's tall pointed one among them), robes whose skirts swing as you walk, bell sleeves, tabards, fur collars, sashes and pouches, and cloaks that stream out behind you. Worn armour grows pauldrons, a crest, a gorget, tassets and cuffs. The townsfolk wear theirs too: aprons, robes, straw hats, a baker's hat, a guard's tabard. New on the Looks page: beard colour, and whether your helmet shows.
  • Live-action combat: nothing is rolled on screen any more. A blow, an arrow or a spell that reaches its mark lands, and your skill against their armour decides how hard. Skill checks in conversation show your score against what's asked before you choose.
  • Towns worth the name: Stonebridge is a walled city with side streets and squares; towns and villages are bigger and built the way RPG towns are. Inside: potions and herbs on the apothecary's shelves, books on the library's, weapons on racks and armour on stands in the smithy, and people at the tavern tables with their bowls and mugs.
  • GeruDoku, one click: Settings → Resource pack → Get GeruDoku fetches the classic pack from its own GitHub release (the desktop app does it by itself the first time it starts). The pack now dresses more of the game: its chainmail, the tabletop's armour (plate, scale, studded, padded and hide, in its iron and leather), some of the tabletop's weapons and gear, and the cloth, leather and metal of the 3D gear.
  • Controller everywhere: the hero maker from the menu (d-pad to choose, left/right to change a look, LB/RB for pages, B back, Start to finish), the story's narration (A to turn the page, B to skip), letters (left/right to turn, B to close) and the journal (LB/RB for its tabs, A to read a letter) all work with a controller now, along with every menu, the pause menu, conversations, shops and the inventory.
  • The in-game hero maker shows your hero in 3D too, and the menu figure stands still unless you turn it (lighter on older computers).

Alpha 0.1.0.1 — First Roll October 2026

The Shadowbloom of Eldenwood begins: a tabletop campaign played in a voxel world, built on DokuQuest 0.9.0.57. Everything DokuQuest does is still here (the world, the building, the creatures, split screen, LAN and online play, the dedicated server), now played by the d20.

  • Make your hero: nine species (human, elf, dwarf, halfling, gnome, dragonborn, orc, tiefling, goliath), twelve classes and ten backgrounds. Ability scores by standard array, point buy or 4d6, skills, and a looks editor with body, skin, hair, beard, eyes, ears, horns, markings, outfits and colours. No skin file needed. Every player on a shared screen makes their own with their own controller, and Heroes on the title screen lets you make one ahead of time.
  • The kit you'd expect: each class starts with its weapons, armour, focus and pack, and gold.
  • The d20 decides: every swing and arrow is an attack roll against armour class, every blow at you is the creature's roll against yours. Weapon dice, critical hits, resistances, sneak attacks and smites. At 0 hit points you fall and roll death saving throws; a friend (or a healing spell) brings you back.
  • Spells: 69 spells from cantrips to 3rd level, cast in real time with spell slots, concentration, saving throws and spell attacks. Z casts, G changes spell.
  • Class and species features: Rage, Second Wind, Lay on Hands, Bardic Inspiration, Wild Shape, Channel Divinity, Flurry of Blows, Shadowstep, Breath Weapon, Adrenaline Rush, Stonecunning and more (B and N).
  • Levels 1 to 20: experience from creatures and quests, hit points, features, ability score improvements and new spells as you grow. Short rests from your character sheet (P); a night in a bed is a long rest.
  • The campaign: towns where the story puts them, people with their own faces and words, conversations with skill checks rolled on screen, quests in stages, a journal (J) of quests, people, places and letters, and a storyteller's narration at the start and the end. The story is shared by everyone playing the world, on one screen or over the network.
  • Shops trade in gold pieces and stock the tabletop's weapons, armour, potions and packs.
  • This first build carries a short test chapter (Brookhollow, the innkeeper Mira and the Bloomrot Crypt) to try the systems with; the full campaign comes in the next update.
  • The old watcher story is gone from these lands.
